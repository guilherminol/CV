# Elevator Pitch — Factored Talent Interview (English)

- **Length:** Max 2 minutes
- **Script length:** 281 words
- **Timing estimate:** ~2:00 at 140 wpm (1:52–2:10 at the 130–150 wpm calm-pace band) — rehearse to land under 2:00 by holding ~140–145 wpm
- **Structure (Factored deck, in order):** Quick intro → Summarized background → Main tech stack → 1 project example → Motivation

<!-- pitch-script-start -->
My name is Guilhermino Lucas, and I'm an AI Engineer at Group Software in Belo Horizonte, Brazil, where I build generative-AI products end to end.

I've worked in tech for five years, since 2021 — and I actually started out as an English instructor, which is where I learned to explain complex ideas clearly. In 2022 I became a developer, building high-performance React interfaces for Rexona at The Brooklyn Brothers, on a 15-person global team across India and the UK. Then, at H&P, I pioneered generative-AI adoption across the company and redesigned 90 relational database tables with a 70% performance increase. Today I build AI products at Group Software, and I study Data Science and Artificial Intelligence at PUC Minas.

My main stack is generative AI and agent architectures: LangChain and LangGraph for agent flows, advanced RAG, and MCP — the Model Context Protocol, where I'm Anthropic-certified — plus Python and FastAPI on the backend and TypeScript and React on the frontend.

One example: Group Verifica, a product that analyzes criminal backgrounds and lawsuits for the condominium and HR markets. I built it end to end — frontend, backend, AI, and database — with LangGraph agent flows querying real-time background-check APIs. What took an analyst nearly a full day now takes under 30 seconds — a ~90% reduction — and the market responded fast: 53 clients tested within 20 days, 72% demonstrated purchase interest, and the product reached R$4,750 in monthly recurring revenue in its first 45 days.

What drives me is applying generative AI to measurably change how businesses operate — a solid software-engineering foundation applied to state-of-the-art AI. My teaching background also makes me a strong communicator and collaborator.

## Rehearsal annotations

### Pause points (breathe between structure beats)

1. After **"…end to end."** — end of intro; drop the pitch, reset, smile.
2. After **"…Artificial Intelligence at PUC Minas."** — end of background; short beat before the stack.
3. After **"…TypeScript and React on the frontend."** — end of stack; breathe before the example.
4. After **"…real-time background-check APIs."** — this is the biggest pause; you're about to land the numbers.
5. Micro-pause after **"…a ~90% reduction —"** — let the number hang in silence for one beat.
6. After **"…in its first 45 days."** — long beat before the motivation close.

### Stress notes

- **Numbers get stress and a beat of silence after them** — this interviewer wants numbers: **five years**, **90 tables**, **70%**, **30 seconds**, **~90%**, **53 clients**, **20 days**, **72%**, **R$4,750**, **45 days**. Slow down into each one, don't rush out of it.
- Pronunciation hints:
  - **Guilhermino** → "ghee-yer-MEE-noo" (hard G, stress on MEE).
  - **LangGraph** → "LANG-graf" (two clean syllables, like "lang" + "graph").
  - **RAG** → "rag," like the fabric — one syllable, no spelling out.
  - **R$4,750** → say aloud as "four thousand, seven hundred and fifty reais."

### Non-verbal (from the deck)

- **Eye contact with the camera**, not the screen — especially on the intro line and the numbers.
- **Hand gestures** on the structure beats (count on fingers for the three background chapters; open hands on the impact numbers).
- **Confident pace:** calm ~140 wpm; if you feel yourself speeding up, the pause list above is your brake.
